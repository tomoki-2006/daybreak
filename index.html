A deadline a year away

I’m in my first semester at a community college in California, studying math. In November 2027 I’ll apply to transfer to UCLA. That was 377 mornings away when I made this: too far to feel real, too important to forget. I wanted a countdown that would still be right tomorrow morning, so I built one that changes every morning.

What Gemini did

Gemini planned the milestones, painted the sky and set up a daily reminder. Daybreak, a small web app, blends the paintings by date and makes a new picture for every morning.

  1. 1 Plan

    I told Gemini my deadline and asked it to work backward to everything that has to be finished by when.

    Two of its lines changed how the deadline reads for me:

    “Fall 2027 is the last term of grades UCLA sees before making admission decisions.”
    “submit by Friday, November 19, 2027—before Thanksgiving week.”

    A year-away deadline starts now. UCLA sees only three semesters and a summer of my grades before it decides, and my first milestone was 27 mornings out.

    Then I asked for five dated milestones and one daily habit. Its first five ended with the deadline itself and left February to August empty, so I asked it to drop that line and add a spring milestone. It came back with “Finish Calc 2”.

    Its plan also told me to submit by November 19, before Thanksgiving week, so the deadline row on my lock screen says “Due · aim for Nov 19”.

    The habit line tells me to solve a problem before checking my phone, and it lives on the one screen I see before I unlock it.

  2. 2 Paint

    Gemini’s draft was beautiful but frozen: it would say “377 mornings left” forever and its milestones would never get checked off. So the job was split. Gemini painted first light, then turned that same painting into night, blue hour and sunrise, so every ridge lines up. Daybreak blends the two nearest paintings for each date, so the sunrise takes exactly as long as the deadline is away, here a year, and makes a new picture for every morning with that day’s numbers.

    Before each picture, Gemini wrote its own long brief for its image model. For the first-light painting, the base of all four: “The top third of the frame is a peaceful, dark twilight sky”, “keeping it open and uncluttered”.

  3. 3 Remind

    I asked Gemini to check the countdown every morning. It set up one daily schedule, “UCLA transfer daily countdown”, at 7:00 until November 30, 2027. It is set to send the mornings left, my next milestone, anything due today or tomorrow, and one small step, in under 40 words.

    Gemini notes that scheduled actions run at about the set time and stop if a usage limit is reached.

On a computer? Scan this with your phone’s camera to open this page there.

Let it change itself

This countdown has one picture online for every morning, named by its date. A Shortcut with four actions fetches today’s and sets it. Setting it up takes about five minutes, once.

  1. Date Current Date
  2. Format Date Date Format: Custom, yyyy-MM-dd
  3. Get Contents of URL https://tomoki-2006.github.io/daybreak/i/uc-transfer-app/Formatted Date.jpg
  4. Set Wallpaper Lock Screen, with Show Preview off

Run it each morning

iOS 27: after building the shortcut, tap Edit › Automation and add a Time of Day trigger (for example 6:00, daily). Then Edit › Info › Privacy and turn on Allow Running When Locked.

iOS 26: Shortcuts › Automation › + › Time of Day (6:00, Daily), add the four actions, choose Run Immediately and turn Notify When Run off.

Turn off Depth Effect and the spatial scene for this Lock Screen. Keep the clock small: the compact clock on iOS 27, or drag the clock’s handle back up on iOS 26.

The online pictures are 1290 × 2796, the size of the larger iPhones; iOS scales them to fit other models. A picture from Set it today is drawn at your screen’s exact size.
